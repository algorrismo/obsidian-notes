## Before you start

> [!important] Sleep needs clarification
> Your reported sleep times equal approximately **4½ hours**. If you meant **12:45 a.m.–5:15 a.m.**, work toward **7–9 hours of sleep**. Include any additional daytime sleep when assessing your total. While sleep remains short, use the reduced starting workload. If you remain exhausted, replace Thursday's lifting with easy walking and mobility.

> [!warning] Previous chest pain
> If the previous pain was unexplained or felt like pressure/tightness during exercise, get medical advice before strenuous training even though it has stopped. Breast/nipple pain that returns or persists, a lump, discharge, or skin changes also needs assessment. Sudden persistent chest pain with breathlessness, sweating, or pain spreading to an arm or jaw needs urgent medical attention. See sources 8 and 9.

The starting calorie target is an estimate. Adjust it using your weight, waist, energy, and training trends. A 1 kg increase alone cannot tell you whether you gained muscle, fat, or water.

Five training days distribute your workload; they do not automatically produce better results than a well-designed four-day routine. Each major muscle group gets at least two weekly training opportunities, consistent with current ACSM guidance. [1]

## How to use the workouts

### Sets, repetitions, and effort

- **One repetition (rep):** one complete movement.
- **One set:** a group of repetitions performed before resting.
- **3 × 8–12:** three sets, each containing 8–12 repetitions.
- **Rest:** the break between sets, not between individual repetitions.
- **Working sets:** challenging sets after the warm-up.
- **Repetitions in reserve (RIR):** how many more clean repetitions you think you could perform.

> [!important] Start with fewer sets
> **First two weeks:** do **two working sets per exercise**, except Thursday, when you do **one working set per exercise**. Afterward, move toward the listed sets only when sleep, soreness, and performance allow. Do not add sets simply because two weeks have passed.

- Finish most sets with approximately **2–3 clean repetitions left**.
- On Thursday, leave about **three repetitions left**.
- Complete all sets of an exercise before moving to the next exercise.
- Lower weights under control; roughly two seconds is a useful learning cue.
- Stop a set when you can no longer maintain your technique.
- Stop an exercise if it causes sharp pain or joint pain.

### Warm-up

1. Walk or cycle easily for about five minutes.
2. Practise the first movement with a light weight.
3. Perform 2–3 gradually heavier warm-up sets before the first challenging exercise.
4. Add a light preparation set before a substantially different movement, especially Romanian deadlifts.

**Warm-up sets do not count toward the working sets below.**

### Why this exercise order?

Larger movements come first while you are fresh. Presses already use your triceps, and rows/pulldowns already use your biceps, so direct arm exercises come later.

Lower-body sessions begin with movements requiring the most strength or coordination. Calves and core come afterward.

There is no special sequence that makes muscles “connect.” Balance comes from training the major movements consistently and recovering between sessions.

**Expected session length:** approximately 50–70 minutes, depending on rest and equipment availability. Thursday should be shorter.

## Muscle coverage across the week

> [!note] Muscles do not all have three parts
> Shoulders have front, side, and rear portions; triceps has three heads; biceps has two. The chest is commonly described as having clavicular and sternocostal portions. The back and lower body contain several muscle groups. Exercises overlap, so each anatomical part does not necessarily require its own exercise. [2–4]

| Area | How the plan covers it |
|---|---|
| Chest | Flat machine pressing plus incline pressing |
| Front shoulders | Chest presses and shoulder press |
| Side shoulders | Lateral raises; shoulder pressing also contributes |
| Rear shoulders | Reverse pec deck and rows |
| Lats | Pulldowns and rows |
| Upper/middle back | Rows and reverse pec deck |
| Lower-back stabilisers | Romanian deadlifts and trunk control during other movements |
| Biceps | Regular curls, hammer curls, and pulling exercises |
| Triceps | Presses, pushdowns, and overhead extensions |
| Forearms/grip | Hammer curls and gripping weights during pulls/hinges |
| Front thighs | Leg press, goblet squat, and leg extension |
| Hamstrings | Leg curls and Romanian deadlifts |
| Glutes | Hip thrusts, squats, leg press, and Romanian deadlifts |
| Calves | Standing calf raises |
| Core | Dead bugs, planks, and stabilisation during other exercises |

These are the **main training emphases**, not claims that every small muscle receives equal isolation work. Cover the main regions across the week; do not add three exercises for every muscle to every session.

Monday's overhead extension gives the triceps a different arm position. Research supports this as a useful option, especially for long-head growth; pushdowns still train the long head too. [5]

## Exercise technique guide

### General breathing and control

**Breathe out during the effort and breathe in during the controlled return.** For example, exhale when pressing away or pulling a pulldown bar toward you. Keep your abdomen gently firm, and avoid holding your breath throughout a set. [6]

Use a comfortable, controlled range of motion. Have a trainer check machine adjustments and unfamiliar exercises in person.

### Machine chest press

**Works:** chest; shoulders and triceps assist.

1. Adjust the seat so the handles are around mid-chest height.
2. Keep your feet planted, wrists straight, and back supported.
3. Press forward smoothly, then return to a comfortable stretch.

**Avoid:** shoulders rolling forward, excessive back arching, elbows flaring directly sideways.  
**Breathe:** out when pressing; in when returning. [10]

### Incline dumbbell chest press

**Works:** chest, with an upper-chest emphasis.

1. Set the bench at a modest incline, around 15–30 degrees.
2. Keep your feet planted and shoulder blades supported against the bench.
3. Lower the dumbbells beside your upper chest, keeping wrists above elbows.
4. Press upward smoothly.

**Avoid:** an almost-upright bench, bouncing, lowering beyond a comfortable shoulder position.  
**Breathe:** out when pressing; in when lowering.

### Chest-supported machine row

**Works:** upper/middle back and lats; biceps assist.

1. Adjust the seat so your chest rests comfortably against the pad.
2. Pull your elbows backward, bringing the handles toward your lower ribs.
3. Return slowly, allowing a controlled reach.

**Avoid:** lifting your chest off the pad, shrugging, jerking with your lower back.  
**Breathe:** out when pulling; in when reaching forward.

### Lat pulldown

**Works:** lats, along the sides of your back; biceps assist.

1. Secure your thighs under the pad and use a comfortable grip.
2. Keep your torso mostly upright with a slight backward lean.
3. Pull your elbows down, bringing the bar toward your upper chest.
4. Return under control.

**Avoid:** pulling behind your neck, swinging backward, forcing the bar to touch your chest.  
**Breathe:** out when pulling down; in during the return.

### Machine shoulder press

**Works:** mainly front shoulders; side shoulders and triceps assist.

1. Adjust the seat so the handles start near shoulder height.
2. Keep your back supported and elbows slightly in front of your torso.
3. Press upward through a comfortable range.

**Avoid:** arching your lower back or pushing through shoulder pain.  
**Breathe:** out when pressing up; in when lowering.

### Dumbbell lateral raise

**Works:** side shoulders.

1. Hold light dumbbells with elbows slightly bent.
2. Raise your arms outward, slightly in front of your body, to about shoulder height.
3. Lower smoothly.

**Avoid:** swinging, shrugging, deliberately turning your thumbs downward.  
**Breathe:** out when raising; in when lowering.

### Reverse pec deck

**Works:** rear shoulders and upper back.

1. Face the chest pad, with the handles around shoulder height.
2. Keep a slight bend in your elbows and open your arms outward.
3. Return slowly.

**Avoid:** throwing your torso backward or forcing your arms far behind you.  
**Breathe:** out when opening; in when returning.

### Dumbbell biceps curl

**Works:** both biceps heads.

1. Stand tall, keeping your upper arms mostly beside your torso.
2. With palms facing upward, curl without swinging.
3. Lower through a comfortable range.

**Avoid:** leaning backward, bending your wrists, moving elbows far forward.  
**Breathe:** out when curling; in when lowering.

### Dumbbell hammer curl

**Works:** biceps, brachialis beneath the biceps, and brachioradialis in the forearm.

Follow the curl technique above, but keep your **palms facing inward** throughout.

**Avoid:** swinging or bending your wrists.  
**Breathe:** out when curling; in when lowering.

### Cable triceps pushdown

**Works:** all three triceps heads.

1. Face the cable with your elbows beside your ribs.
2. Straighten your elbows to push the attachment down.
3. Return without allowing your upper arms to swing forward.

**Avoid:** using body weight to slam the cable downward.  
**Breathe:** out when pushing down; in when returning.

### Overhead cable triceps extension

**Works:** triceps, with the long head in a more stretched position.

1. Ask your trainer to show you a comfortable cable height and stance.
2. Hold your upper arms comfortably overhead and keep your abdomen gently firm.
3. Bend your elbows to lower the attachment behind your head, then straighten them smoothly.
4. Use a light weight while learning the movement.

**Avoid:** arching your back, swinging your upper arms, forcing a painful shoulder position.  
**Breathe:** out when straightening; in when bending.  
**Alternative:** triceps pushdown if the overhead position is uncomfortable.

### Leg press

**Works:** front thighs and glutes.

1. Place your feet approximately shoulder-width apart.
2. Keep your whole foot on the platform and your hips/back supported.
3. Lower to a comfortable depth **before your pelvis starts curling off the pad**.
4. Push through your whole foot, keeping knees following your toes.

**Avoid:** knees collapsing inward, heels lifting, snapping your knees backward.  
**Breathe:** in when lowering; out when pressing. [11]

### Goblet squat

**Works:** front thighs and glutes.

1. Hold one dumbbell close to your chest.
2. Stand around shoulder-width, with toes slightly outward if comfortable.
3. Bend your knees and hips together, keeping the whole foot grounded.
4. Stand up without losing your balance.

**Avoid:** forcing depth that makes your heels rise or your back lose control.  
**Breathe:** in before/during the descent; out as you stand.

Your knees can move beyond your toes when appropriate for your proportions; your shins do not need to stay perfectly vertical. [12]

### Dumbbell Romanian deadlift

**Works:** hamstrings and glutes; lower-back muscles stabilise.

1. Hold dumbbells close to the front of your thighs.
2. Slightly bend your knees, then move your hips backward.
3. Lower the dumbbells close to your legs until you feel a hamstring stretch while keeping your back position steady.
4. Bring your hips forward to stand.

**Avoid:** trying to reach the floor, rounding your back, turning the movement into a squat.  
**Breathe:** in while lowering; out while standing. [13]

### Seated leg curl

**Works:** hamstrings at the back of your thighs.

1. Align your knee with the machine's pivot and secure the thigh pad.
2. Position the lower pad above your heel, following the machine's instructions.
3. Bend your knees, then return slowly.

**Avoid:** lifting your hips or letting the weight stack crash.  
**Breathe:** out when curling; in when returning.

### Leg extension

**Works:** quadriceps at the front of your thighs.

1. Align your knees with the machine's pivot and place the pad above your ankles.
2. Straighten your knees smoothly, then lower under control.

**Avoid:** kicking the weight, lifting your hips, snapping into the top position.  
**Breathe:** out when straightening; in when lowering.

### Hip-thrust machine

**Works:** mainly gluteus maximus.

1. Have your trainer show you the machine's belt/pad and safety mechanism.
2. Keep your feet planted and lift your hips until your torso and thighs are approximately aligned.
3. Contract your glutes while keeping your ribs controlled.

**Avoid:** lifting higher by arching your lower back.  
**Breathe:** out when lifting; in when lowering.

### Standing calf raise

**Works:** calves.

1. Keep the balls of your feet supported and hold the machine/rail for balance.
2. Raise your heels and pause briefly.
3. Lower into a comfortable stretch.

**Avoid:** bouncing, rolling onto the outside of your feet, forcing your knees backward.  
**Breathe:** out when rising; in when lowering.

### Dead bug

**Works:** core control.

1. Lie on your back with arms upward and hips/knees bent.
2. Slowly extend the opposite arm and leg without letting your lower back arch.
3. Return and alternate sides.

**Avoid:** extending farther than you can control or holding your breath.  
**Breathe:** out when extending; in when returning.

### Forearm plank

**Works:** core stability.

1. Place your elbows beneath your shoulders.
2. Keep your body approximately straight and your abdomen gently firm.
3. End the hold before your lower back starts sagging.

**Avoid:** sagging, lifting your hips excessively, or holding your breath.  
**Breathe:** normally throughout.

## Progressive overload

**Increase repetitions first, then increase the weight.**

For an exercise prescribed as **3 × 8–12**, an example progression using the same weight is:

| Session | Set 1 | Set 2 | Set 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | 10 | 9 | 8 |
| Later | 11 | 10 | 10 |
| Later | 12 | 12 | 12 |

When all sets reach the top of the repetition range with good form and about **two repetitions still available**, repeat that performance at the next session. Then increase the weight by the smallest manageable increment.

- After an increase, dropping back to around 8–10 repetitions is normal for an 8–12 range.
- If you cannot reach the bottom of the range with good form, the increase was too large.
- Apply the same rule to the other repetition ranges.
- You do not have to increase weights every week.
- During the reduced starting phase, apply the rule to the number of sets you are actually doing.

### When to take an easier week

If several exercises get worse across consecutive sessions and you feel unusually tired, take an easier week:

- Perform roughly **half the usual sets**.
- Use comfortable weights and leave several repetitions in reserve.
- Prioritise sleep and regular meals.
- Resume normal training when recovery improves.

## Six-month progression

| Month | Main task |
|---|---|
| **1** | Learn technique, improve sleep, establish food portions, and start a log. Use reduced sets initially. |
| **2** | Build toward the listed sets if recovering well. Apply the repetition-then-weight rule. |
| **3** | Continue the same exercises. Review waist, weight, and strength trends; adjust food only when needed. |
| **4** | Keep progressing. If one muscle group has stalled despite good recovery, consider adding one weekly set for it. |
| **5** | Maintain consistency around university. Use an easier week if fatigue accumulates. |
| **6** | Compare measurements, photos, and performance with Month 1. Plan the next phase from actual results. |

These are priorities, not deadlines for a particular weight increase or a guaranteed transformation.

## Mind–muscle connection and locking

| Term | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Squeezing a muscle | Deliberately contracting it | Briefly focus on the working muscle while controlling the movement |
| Bracing your core | Firming your abdomen to stabilise your torso | Gently tighten around your abdomen and continue breathing |
| Locking a joint | Finishing near a straight elbow/knee position | Finish under control; avoid forcefully snapping backward |

Normal controlled straightening is different from forcing a joint beyond its comfortable range.

The mind–muscle connection means paying attention to the muscle doing the work. It can help you learn, but an intense burn or a dramatic squeeze is not required on every repetition.

Prioritise **stable technique, a useful range of motion, appropriate effort, and gradual progress**.

## Nutrition and fat loss

Building muscle while losing fat is possible, but the amount varies. A structured plan does not guarantee a specific transformation. [14]

Chest exercises cannot selectively remove chest fat, and abdominal exercises cannot selectively remove belly fat. Body-fat changes happen across the body. [15]

### Starting daily targets

| Item | Starting target |
|---|---|
| Calories | **About 2,400 kcal/day as a trial** |
| Protein | **140–170 g/day; aim around 150 g** |
| Fat | **Approximately 60–75 g/day** |
| Carbohydrates | Remaining calories, from rice, roti, oats, fruit, lentils, etc. |

**2,400 kcal is an estimate, not a measured requirement.** Your walking/activity and actual food portions are unclear. Use 2–3 weeks of consistent tracking to decide whether it fits.

The protein target is approximately **1.6–2.0 g/kg**. [16]

### Your current breakfast

This plan assumes “shiya-seeht” means **chia seeds soaked in water**, and “akhrot” means **walnuts**.

Two eggs provide roughly **12–14 g protein**. Small servings of chia, walnuts, and pumpkin seeds add useful nutrients but relatively little protein. Keep them if you enjoy them, and add a dependable protein source such as milk.

### Example day using familiar foods

**Measurement rule:** rice, chicken, fish, and lentils below are **cooked weights/portions**. Oats are weighed **dry**. Meat/fish weights refer to the edible portion without bones.

| Meal | Example | Approximate protein |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | 2 eggs, 250 ml milk, 60 g oats, a small portion of soaked chia and walnuts | 28–33 g |
| Lunch | 200 g rice, 120 g chicken **or** 150 g fish, ½ cup thick dal, vegetables | 42–55 g |
| Snack | 250 ml milk, 40 g roasted chickpeas, a banana | 16–19 g |
| Dinner | 200 g rice, 120 g chicken **or** 150 g fish, ½ cup thick dal, vegetables | 42–55 g |

This is a **portion template**, not an exact 2,400 kcal calculation. Fish type, milk fat, dal thickness, and cooking method change the totals. Check labels and measure your usual portions for a week or two to bring the day near your target.

### When fish or chicken is unavailable

- **50 g dry soy chunks**, cooked afterward, often provide around **24–26 g protein**; check the packet. Add an egg if needed.
- **Three eggs plus one cup of thick cooked lentils** provide roughly **35–38 g protein**.
- Milk can fill smaller gaps. Yogurt is optional.

These replacements have different calorie totals; adjust the rice portion when needed. A cup of cooked lentils contains about **18 g protein**, but a cup of watery dal contains less. [17]

### Oil, sugar, and hydration

- Cooking without added oil is fine if your overall diet provides enough fat.
- Fish, nuts, seeds, and modest amounts of unsaturated oils can fit a healthy diet. [18]
- Fruit and plain milk are suitable despite containing natural sugars.
- Avoiding added sugar and oil alone does not guarantee fat loss; overall intake matters. [19]
- Drink regularly and bring water to the gym, especially in hot weather.

### Meal timing for 2:30 p.m. workouts

1. Have lunch around **12:00–1:00 p.m.**, depending on digestion.
2. If hungry near training, have a small snack such as a banana.
3. Have the next protein-containing meal/snack afterward.
4. Spread protein across **3–4 meals**; there is no need to rush a shake within minutes of finishing.

Keep protein intake similar on rest days.

### Adjusting calories

Compare trends after **2–3 consistent weeks**, allowing for initial water changes.

| Trend | Action |
|---|---|
| Losing roughly 0.2–0.4 kg/week, strength reasonably stable | Continue |
| Weight stable, waist shrinking, strength improving | Continue |
| Weight and waist rising consistently | Reduce around 150–200 kcal/day |
| Weight and waist unchanged, with no useful progress | Check portions and consistency, then consider a small reduction |
| Losing more than roughly 0.5 kg/week after initial water changes, with poor energy/performance | Add around 150–200 kcal/day |

## Cardio

- Start with **15–20 minutes of brisk walking or easy cycling after two upper-body sessions**.
- Add a third session if recovery is good.
- Use a pace where you can speak in sentences.
- Gradually work toward **150 minutes of moderate activity weekly**, including brisk walking outside the gym. Build up gradually. [20]
- If tracking steps, learn your current average first, then add roughly **1,000 daily steps** when manageable.

Exhausting intervals are unnecessary at this stage.

## Sleep, rest days, and soreness

### Sleep

Adults your age should get at least seven hours; aim for roughly **7–9 hours**. [7]

If you must wake at **5:15 a.m.**, being asleep around **9:45–10:15 p.m.** gives approximately 7–7½ hours. Move bedtime earlier gradually and allow time to fall asleep. Include any additional sleep after the early wake-up when calculating your total.

### Rest days

- Take an easy walk if comfortable.
- Do a few minutes of gentle mobility if helpful.
- Eat normally and maintain protein intake.
- Keep Wednesday and Friday free of hard training.

### Soreness

Mild soreness can follow unfamiliar exercise and may be strongest around **24–72 hours** afterward. Gentle movement is usually reasonable. Reduce or postpone hard training if soreness changes your movement. [21]

**Sharp pain, joint pain, significant swelling, or worsening symptoms are not ordinary training soreness.** Stop the provoking exercise and seek assessment.

## Supplements

| Supplement | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Whey protein | Optional when food leaves you short of your protein target; check protein per serving |
| Creatine monohydrate | Optional; **3–5 g daily** is a usual approach |
| BCAAs | Low priority if you already consume enough complete protein |
| Mass gainer | Poor fit for the current goal; makes extra calories easy to consume |
| Fat burners / testosterone boosters | Skip; unnecessary for this plan |
| High-stimulant pre-workout | Skip for now, especially while sleep is limited |

Creatine does not require a loading phase. It can increase scale weight through water stored in muscle, so record when you start it. [22]

Whey is a convenient food supplement. Sufficient dietary protein already supplies the amino acids found in BCAA products. [16]

Prioritise regular meals in your budget. If buying supplements, choose products with credible independent quality testing.

## Progress tracking

| What | Frequency | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Body weight | 3–7 mornings/week | After using the toilet, before eating; compare weekly averages |
| Waist | Weekly | At belly-button level, relaxed, after a normal breath out |
| Photos | Every four weeks | Front, side, back; same lighting and distance |

A smaller waist with improving strength is encouraging even if scale weight changes slowly.

## Common mistakes

- Adding the old PPL workouts on top of this plan.
- Taking every set to the point where another repetition is impossible.
- Increasing weights while shortening the movement or swinging.
- Adding extra chest exercises to try to burn chest fat.
- Changing exercises every week.
- Cutting rice drastically while leaving protein too low.
- Treating seeds and nuts as replacements for substantial protein servings.
- Using soreness, sweat, or a temporary pump as proof of growth.
- Expecting supplements to compensate for insufficient sleep.
- Judging progress from one weigh-in.
- Adding three exercises for every muscle because of the mistaken idea that all muscles have three parts.
